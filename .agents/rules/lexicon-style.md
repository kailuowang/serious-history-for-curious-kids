# Lexicon & Prose Style Rules

Before writing or editing any file in `manuscript/`, read `syllabus/style-anchor.md`.

## 1. Earn Every Concept Before Naming It
- Never drop an abstract term cold. Show the physical problem first, show how humans solved it, and only then introduce the term in bold (e.g., **tax**, **contract**, **interest**, **keystone**).
- Introduce as many newly earned systemic concepts as the chapter's causal chain genuinely needs (no rigid numeric cap—just avoid dumping a vocabulary list), and reserve bold formatting strictly for those newly earned concepts.
- Explain unfamiliar units or concrete engineering terms (e.g., *hectare*, *ration*, *shearing*) in plain everyday words the first time they appear.

## 2. Banned Vocabulary & AI Tics
- **Ban Latinate academic abstractions:** Do not use words like *stratification, hegemony, agrarian, socio-political, mercantilism, institutionalize, bureaucracy* (unless explicitly introducing *bureaucracy* via desk-scribes), *proliferation, catalyst, paradigm, multifaceted*.
- **Translate abstractions into physical actions:** Instead of "socioeconomic stratification increased," write "landowners with three grain silos could hire landless neighbors to dig their ditches."
- **Ban vague adjectives:** Replace *massive, prosperous, complex* with physical quantities.
- **Ban unexplained historian era shorthand:** Never drop specialist period labels (*Ur III*, *Old Babylonian*, *Uruk III*, *Late Uruk*, *Middle Kingdom*, *Late Antiquity*) cold in prose, photo captions, or diagrams. Replace shorthand with plain place + date phrasing (*"in Ur and Babylon between 2100 and 1700 BCE"*) and anchor dates in human scale (*"more than five thousand years ago"*, *"a thousand years after Uruk's first tablets"*) so a 9-year-old always knows how old something is and how far apart two events sit.
- **Ban stock LLM transitions and puffery:** Never write *Furthermore, Moreover, Crucially, It is important to note, A testament to, Tapestry, Delve, Nestled, Bustling, Monumental feat*.
- **Ban "kid-book" condescension:** Never use exclamation marks for fake excitement, rhetorical baby talk ("Can you imagine?!"), or fictional child mascots. Address the reader as an intellectual peer who simply hasn't learned the specialized vocabulary yet.

## 3. Sentence Rhythm & Structure
- Keep average sentence length between 14 and 18 words. Mix crisp 8-word sentences with clear 22-word causal sentences.
- Prefer active voice and concrete subjects (farmers, stonecutters, merchants, tax collectors) over abstract nouns as subjects.

## 4. Unfolding-Mystery Causal Flow (Chapter & Unit Level)
- Treat every chapter—and every smaller section/unit inside it—like **unfolding a mystery**:
  `Physical Problem / Mystery → Clues & Constraints → Human Mechanism / Payoff → New Problem Triggered`.
- **Zero Dropped Threads:** Whenever a paragraph introduces a difficulty or question (e.g., "No single family could pull that off" or "What stopped someone from scratching out a number?"), the **very next beat** must either solve it directly OR explicitly frame it as the cliffhanger question that the next section immediately answers.
- **Zero Causal Teleports:** Never skip over an unsolved problem with a hand-waving summary transition (e.g., "Working together under a shared schedule paid off..." before explaining *how* people got neighbors onto a shared schedule).
- **Strict Cause-Before-Effect Ordering:** Never show a large-scale outcome (e.g., a city of 40,000) as already achieved before explaining the mechanism that made it possible, unless explicitly framed as the puzzle/prize we are about to explain.

## 5. Avoid Grand Idiosyncratic Claims
- Present each chapter's newly earned concepts (`surplus`, `tax`, `ledger`, `contract`, etc.) strictly as concrete solutions and historical examples from that specific time and place.
- Never inflate a chapter's tools into a sweeping universal theory or personal framework—avoid phrases like *"the basic operating kit of all civilization"*, *"the N pillars of society"*, or sweeping claims that *"every city everywhere"* ran on the same checklist.
